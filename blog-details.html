5 Batting Drills Every Cricketer Must Master

After 25 years in professional cricket as a player and coach, I've identified the 5 drills that separate good batsmen from great ones. These aren't just exercises — they're the fundamental pillars of elite batting that I've refined through thousands of coaching sessions at EliteStrike.

"The quality of your practice determines the quality of your performance. Every drill has a purpose — know the 'why' before you pick up the bat." — Arjun Sharma

1. Shadow Batting Drill

Shadow batting is the most underrated drill in cricket. Pick up your bat without a ball, stand in front of a mirror and play every shot in your repertoire. The key is to move your feet first, then swing the bat — never the other way around.

Start each session with 100 shadow shots across all positions — front foot drives, back foot pulls, cuts, and glances. Your muscle memory will thank you when you're facing a 140 km/h delivery under pressure.

2. Throw-Down Sessions

Throw-down sessions are a staple at EliteStrike for a very good reason. Having a coach or training partner throw balls at a specific length and line for extended periods forces you to commit to playing each delivery on its merit.

  • Start with 20 consecutive forward defense shots on full length
  • Progress to 20 back-foot punches on good length
  • Finish with 20 attacking front-foot drives
  • Repeat 3 sets without taking a break between shots

3. The Batting Tee Drill

The batting tee is not just for beginners. Even professional cricketers use batting tees to isolate specific technical errors. At EliteStrike, we use tees to correct common faults like:

  • Head falling away at point of contact
  • Hard hands instead of soft hands for placement shots
  • Swinging across the line
  • Poor weight transfer through the shot

4. The Short-Pitch Reaction Drill

Modern cricket demands batsmen to be exceptional against short-pitched bowling. This drill uses a bowling machine set to deliver balls at variable heights between chest and throat height. The aim is to:

First, build instinctive reactions to the bouncer. Then, develop the ability to pick the ball early and choose between the hook shot, duck, sway, or the safe option of taking it on the gloves and letting it drop.

5. The Two-Ball Concentrate Drill

This is my personal favourite — and it's the drill I use with every player who joins our Elite Pathway program. Place two balls side by side on a bowling machine. Set the machine to alternate between two different speeds. The batsman must identify which ball is coming, make a split-second decision, and play the correct shot.

This drill develops concentration, decisiveness and the ability to process information under pressure — the exact skills needed when a crafty bowler tries to trick you with variations.

"Consistency comes from repetition, and great technique comes from purposeful repetition. These 5 drills, done properly, will transform your batting within 3 months." — Arjun Sharma

How to Structure Your Practice

Combine all 5 drills into a structured 90-minute practice session. Begin with shadow batting as a warm-up (15 mins), move to the tee drill for technique work (20 mins), then throw-downs (25 mins), short-pitch reactions (15 mins) and finish with the two-ball concentrate drill (15 mins).
